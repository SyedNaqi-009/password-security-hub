What Is Password Entropy and Why Does It Matter?

Published: Sept 2026 • By PSH Security Labs • 8 Min Read

Entropy is the gold standard metric used by cryptographers to quantify the randomness and unpredictability of a password. Rather than relying on subjective ratings, entropy provides a rigorous mathematical count of how many guesses an attacker needs.

The Mathematical Formula

Password entropy ($E$) is measured in bits and is calculated using the formula:

E = L × log2(R)

Where:

  • L = Length of the password (number of characters).
  • R = Size of the character pool (e.g. 26 for lowercase, 62 for alphanumeric, 95 for full ASCII).

What Does the Number of Bits Mean?

If a password has $N$ bits of entropy, an attacker must make on average $2^{N-1}$ guesses to find it. Because every added bit doubles the difficulty:

  • 30 bits: $2^{30} \approx 10^9$ guesses (Cracked in seconds on a GPU).
  • 60 bits: $2^{60} \approx 1.15 \times 10^{18}$ guesses (Resistant to online and standard offline attacks).
  • 100+ bits: $2^{100} \approx 1.26 \times 10^{30}$ guesses (Immune to all foreseeable brute-force attacks).
